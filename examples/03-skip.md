# Example 3: Saying no (Source of Sources)

An illustrative session. The expert and queries are made up.

Source of Sources says it bans anyone who pitches off topic, even once, so the skill holds the line.

---

**Dana:** Can you help me answer these two from today's Source of Sources email?

1. "What's the best color for kitchen garbage bags, and why?"
2. "Looking for a source to comment on whether small law firms should use AI for document review."

**Claude:** Your profile: commercial real estate law, 14 years, partner at a 12-lawyer firm. You haven't listed legal tech as a topic.

Neither of these passes the fit test:

- **#1, garbage bag colors.** Fails the "expert gap" check. Anyone can answer a consumer opinion question, so an expert's answer adds nothing. Skip.
- **#2, AI for document review.** It's close to your work, but your profile doesn't include legal tech, and you haven't said you've chosen or run a document-review tool. Skip, unless you have.

On Source of Sources, one off-topic reply can mean a permanent ban, so I won't help draft either of these as they stand.

**Dana:** Actually, we tested two AI review tools last year and dropped one after it missed a lease clause.

**Claude:** That's firsthand experience, so #2 now passes. Want to add "legal tech: AI tools in a small firm" to your profile, with that trial as the proof?

**Dana:** Yes, add it.

**Claude:** Done. Because this is Source of Sources, I'll work in **coach mode**: I'll interview you, give your own words back in order, and you write the email. Also check whether your firm or bar rules limit what you can say publicly about vendors before you send.

First question: what made you drop the tool that missed the clause?

---

**What to notice:** the garbage bag question was a skip with no exceptions. The legal tech question was a skip until the user gave firsthand proof. Claude then offered to update the profile instead of quietly widening it, used the platform's mode, and flagged that Dana is in a regulated field.
